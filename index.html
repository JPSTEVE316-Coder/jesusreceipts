Receipt 1 of 6 — Exhibit A

The Paper Trail

The New Testament is the best-attested text of the ancient world — thousands of copies, the earliest fragments within decades of the writings. No other ancient text even comes close.

EXHIBIT A · THE PAPER TRAILFILE GRADE: A–
1
COUNT: ~5,700 GREEK · ~24,000 TOTAL
~5,700 Greek manuscripts (fragments to complete New Testaments), ~10,000 Latin, ~9,300+ in other ancient versions (Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, Ethiopic, Slavic). The count keeps growing — new papyri surface regularly.
2
GAP: DECADES, NOT A MILLENNIUM
Original-to-earliest-copy gap: ~25–100 years for the New Testament, versus ~800–1,000 years for Tacitus' Annals — which survives in two principal medieval manuscripts (9th-century Fulda, 11th-century Monte Cassino).
3
THE STANDARD THEY ALREADY ACCEPT
Historians call Caesar's Gallic Wars reliable on 10 copies with a 1,000-year gap. The New Testament has 2,400× the copies with ~1/10 the gap. Apply the same standard to both; get a devastating result.
4
P52 — JOHN, COPIED WITHIN DECADES
A credit-card-sized fragment of John's Gospel (John 18) found in Egypt, dated by handwriting to ~AD 100–175. Someone in Egypt was copying John within decades of the writings. The honest caveat too: the John Rylands Library itself now says recent research points "nearer to 200 AD." Even AD 200 is ~110 years vs. ~1,000 for Caesar.
5
COMPLETE CODICES BY ~AD 350
Sinaiticus preserves the complete New Testament; Vaticanus nearly all of it (breaks off at Hebrews 9:14). Compare: our earliest complete Caesar is from the 9th century.
6
THE "VARIANTS" SCARE, DEFUSED
~300,000 variants — Bart Ehrman's own count, and we quote it before he does. But ~70% are spelling differences; strip spelling, word order, and harmonizations, and ~400 disputed words remain in ~40 lines of ~20,000 (Metzger's comparative illustration, not a census). Scholars recover 97–99% of the original text with certainty — and no textual variant affects any cardinal doctrine. Metzger, Wallace, and even Ehrman agree on that. The variants are visible because we have so many copies to compare. More copies = more certainty, not less.
"There is no body of ancient literature in the world which enjoys such a wealth of good textual attestation as the New Testament."— F.F. BRUCE, THE NEW TESTAMENT DOCUMENTS: ARE THEY RELIABLE?
WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOESN'T COVER — A well-preserved text isn't automatically a true one; the Iliad is well-preserved too. This exhibit only files one thing: we have what they wrote. Did it happen? That's Exhibits D, E, and F.

Receipt 2 of 6 — Exhibit B

Hostile Witnesses

Josephus. Tacitus. Pliny. Suetonius. None of them were fans — and all of them write down the same footprint: a real man, crucified under Pilate.

EXHIBIT B · HOSTILE WITNESSESFILE GRADE: A–
1
JOSEPHUS NAMES JESUS TWICE
A Jewish priest turned Roman citizen, writing ~AD 93 for a Roman audience — not a Christian. The James passage (Antiquities 20.9.1) casually refers to "James, the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ." Accepted as authentic across the spectrum, skeptics included.
2
THE TESTIMONIUM — LEAD WITH THE CAVEAT
Yes, Christian scribes touched up Josephus' longer Jesus passage (18.3.3) — we show that first, not last. Strip the three obvious interpolations and the authentic core reads exactly like Josephus: neutral, factual, slightly annoyed. Partial authenticity, not total forgery, is the majority position.
3
TACITUS RECORDS THE CRUCIFIXION
Rome's greatest historian writes that "Christus… suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus" (Annals 15.44, ~AD 116) — in the same breath as calling Christianity "a most mischievous superstition." This is no Christian plant. (Small precision: Tacitus says "procurator" — the Pilate stone in Exhibit C reads prefect.)
4
PLINY TO THE EMPEROR, ~AD 112
An imperial governor's official correspondence reports Christians singing hymns to Christ "as to a god." The divinity claim isn't a fourth-century invention — it's in Rome's paperwork ~80 years after the crucifixion.
5
ROME NOTICED WITHIN ~20 YEARS
By ~AD 49 (Suetonius / Claudius), Jewish-Christian disturbances in Rome reach the emperor's ear — matching the expulsion in Acts 18:2. By AD 64, Nero persecutes an "immense multitude" (Tacitus). Movements built on nothing don't get imperial attention that fast.
6
NO ANCIENT CRITIC EVER DENIED HE EXISTED
The ancients who hated Christianity — Celsus, Porphyry, Julian — argued about who Jesus was, never whether he was. The "he never existed" position is a 19th-century invention: Dupuis/Volney; Bruno Bauer first affirmed it academically.
"Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome."— TACITUS, ANNALS 15.44 (CHURCH–BRODRIBB)
WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOESN'T COVER — These witnesses don't prove the resurrection or the divinity of Christ — and we never say they do. They file the footprint: a real person, executed under Pilate, whose followers multiplied across the empire within a generation.

Receipt 3 of 6 — Exhibit C

Dirt Don't Lie

The Pilate stone. Caiaphas' bone box. A crucified man's heel bone. John's five porticoes. The ground keeps confirming the background. Legends botch incidental details. These don't.

EXHIBIT C · DIRT DON'T LIEFILE GRADE: A–
1
THE PILATE STONE — 1961, CAESAREA
"…Pontius Pilatus… Praefectus Iudaeae" — Pilate's name, his correct title, and his window (AD 26–36), carved in Latin on a theater staircase, now in the Israel Museum. Before 1961, skeptics could note that Pilate appeared in no archaeological record. They can no longer say that.
2
THE STONE CORRECTS OUR OWN SOURCES
It reads prefect, not procurator — the title Tacitus used. We print this because we don't cherry-pick: the archaeology is the authority, and it refined the literary record.
3
THE CAIAPHAS OSSUARY — 1990, JERUSALEM
"Joseph son of Caiaphas" — very likely the high priest's family tomb. The inscription gives the name, not the title, so "very likely" is the honest word, not "certain." The man who sent Jesus to Pilate very likely has a labeled bone box.
4
THE CRUCIFIED MAN — 1968, JERUSALEM
Yehohanan's heel bone with a 4.5-inch (11.5 cm) iron nail driven through it — the tip bent after striking a knot in the wood; the foot was removed with the nail and wood still attached. The first skeletal evidence of Roman crucifixion ever discovered. Confirms that nailing was a real Roman execution practice in 1st-century Judea — consistent with the Gospel accounts.
5
JOHN'S PORTICOES — FIVE OF THEM
John 5:2 describes the Pool of Bethesda with five porticoes — a detail some scholars long considered symbolic or invented. Excavation found exactly that. And the Pool of Siloam (John 9) surfaced in 2004. This isn't a closed 19th-century file — the confirmations keep coming.
6
WHAT WE LEFT OUT — ON PURPOSE
The James ossuary (authenticity unresolved). The Nazareth inscription (a 2020 marble-isotope study traced the marble to Kos, actively undercutting the Jesus-tomb theory). The Shroud (its own debate). Printing what we excluded is the strongest trust signal in this file: a brand built on receipts doesn't file disputed ones.
ACROSS SIX DECADES OF RELEVANT FINDS, THE PATTERN RUNS TOWARD CONFIRMATION — NO EXCAVATED FIND HAS FALSIFIED A GOSPEL BACKGROUND DETAIL.
WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOESN'T COVER — The dirt proves the stage, not the play. Archaeology can't excavate a resurrection. What's filed here: the Gospel writers described a real world with precision — the necessary foundation for taking their central claims seriously.

Receipt 4 of 6 — Exhibit D

The Early Creed

1 Corinthians 15:3–7 — the claim that Jesus died, was buried, rose, and appeared — was already a fixed, memorized creed within months to a few years of the crucifixion. Skeptical scholars grant the dating.

EXHIBIT D · THE EARLY CREEDFILE GRADE: A–
1
RECEIVED / DELIVERED — TECHNICAL TERMS
Paul uses paralambanō ("received") and paradidōmi ("delivered") — the standard ancient idiom for transmitting tradition. He's flagging: I'm quoting, not composing.
2
IT DOESN'T SOUND LIKE PAUL
The passage is tight, parallel, rhythmic — nothing like his normal prose — with a concentration of phrases uncharacteristic of Paul and Semitic coloring. Scholars read it as a pre-Pauline fragment embedded in the letter.
3
LIKELY COINED BEFORE PAUL JOINED
Verse 8's "last of all… to me also" appears to exclude Paul from "all the apostles" in v. 7 — so the creed was probably coined while the apostolic circle was considered closed: before his conversion. Honest caveat: a Habermas-school inference, not standard critical fare.
4
PAUL MET THE NAMED EYEWITNESSES
The two apostles the creed names — Cephas and James — are exactly the two Paul spent fifteen days with on his first Jerusalem visit (Galatians 1:18–19). The leading hypothesis is that this is where he received it.
5
"MOST OF WHOM ARE STILL ALIVE"
"More than five hundred brothers at one time, most of whom are still alive" — written ~AD 53–55, roughly 25 years after the events. We read that as an invitation to check; the reading is contested (Ehrman's demographic objection is on the record). Either way: an impossibly early, checkable claim.
6
SKEPTICAL SCHOLARS GRANT THE DATING
Lüdemann (atheist): "the elements in the tradition are to be dated to the first two years after the crucifixion… not later than three years." Goulder (atheist): "goes back at least to what Paul was taught when he was converted, a couple of years after the crucifixion." Ehrman (agnostic): "This passage almost certainly contains a pre-Pauline confession, or creed, of some kind." Even the mythicist Richard Carrier concedes the dating.
WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOESN'T COVER — This files the claim's age, not the event. But "the legend grew over decades" is done — there were no decades. The belief was fixed, memorized, and circulating while the eyewitnesses were still alive to object.

Receipt 5 of 6 — Exhibit E

The Empty Tomb

Jesus was buried in a known tomb — and within days, that tomb was empty. Even his enemies' earliest counter-story doesn't deny it; it tries to explain it.

EXHIBIT E · THE EMPTY TOMBFILE GRADE: B+
1
BURIED BY A NAMED MEMBER OF THE ENEMY'S COUNCIL
Joseph of Arimathea (Mark 15:43) — a named member of the very council that condemned him. Jerusalem was a small city and he was known. You don't invent that kind of burial hero if the burial never happened.
2
WOMEN FOUND IT — ALL FOUR GOSPELS AGREE
In first-century Judea, women's testimony was discounted — Josephus says it wasn't even permitted as witnesses in court (Antiquities 4.219). Habermas: "Since the testimony of women was not allowed in a law court, why would they be cited as witnesses unless that is what happened?" A fabricated story designed to convince would be unlikely to choose them.
3
THE OTHER SIDE'S STORY CONFESSES IT
Matthew 28:11–15: the chief priests' explanation is that "his disciples came by night and stole him away while we were asleep." You don't invent a stolen-body story for an occupied tomb — the opposition's first move admits the tomb was empty. (The guard detail appears only in Matthew and is widely seen as Matthean apologetic. We don't lean on it.)
4
PREACHED WHERE THE TOMB WAS
The resurrection was proclaimed in Jerusalem — the very city of the tomb — within weeks. If a body were in that tomb, the movement dies in days: producing it ends Christianity before it starts. Nobody produced one.
5
"BY FAR MOST EXEGETES"
Resurrection specialist Jacob Kremer's summary of the scholarship: "By far most exegetes hold firmly to the reliability of the biblical statements concerning the empty tomb." Habermas' mid-2000s survey put support at ~75% — the majority framing is defensible.
6
ONE EARLY TRADITION, NOT A BOLT-ON
Mark's pre-Gospel passion source doesn't end at burial — the empty-tomb account is tied to the burial narrative verbally and grammatically. And the earliest creed says "he was buried" (1 Cor 15:4, Exhibit D) — for a first-century Jew, burial-and-raising language means the tomb is empty.
"By far most exegetes hold firmly to the reliability of the biblical statements concerning the empty tomb."— JACOB KREMER, DIE OSTEREVANGELIEN (1977)
WHAT THIS RECEIPT DOESN'T COVER — An empty tomb alone proves a disappearance that demands explanation, not a resurrection. Theft, wrong tomb, and swoon all fail on the evidence — but the positive case is Exhibit F's. E files the empty tomb; F fills it with meaning.

Receipt 6 of 6 — Exhibit F

The Witnesses

Cowards became martyrs. A skeptic brother converted. Christianity's worst enemy flipped. Because, they said, they saw him alive. People don't die for what they know is a lie.

EXHIBIT F · THE WITNESSESFILE GRADE: A–
1
THE LIST IS EARLY AND CHECKABLE
The appearance roster (1 Cor 15:5–8) dates within ~5 years of the crucifixion and names living witnesses — "most of whom are still alive." That's not legend; that's a dare.
2
COWARDS → MARTYRS
Friday they fled (Mark 14:50); Peter denied him three times. Within weeks they're proclaiming the resurrection in Jerusalem through imprisonment, beatings, and execution. Something happened between Friday and Pentecost.
3
THE SKEPTIC CONVERTED
His own family tried to restrain him — "he's out of his mind" (Mark 3:21). His brothers didn't believe (John 7:5). After the crucifixion, James leads the Jerusalem church — and Josephus records that James, the brother of Jesus, was stoned around AD 62. What converts a skeptical brother?
4
THE ENEMY CONVERTED
Paul, the movement's most violent enemy — hunting Christians with arrest warrants (Acts 9) — becomes its most prolific missionary: 13 of 27 New Testament books traditionally attributed to him (critical scholars accept only 7 as undisputed), through shipwrecks, beatings, and imprisonment. An enemy doesn't flip for a story he knows is false.
5
500 AT ONCE KILLS THE HALLUCINATION THEORY
Grief hallucinations are real — individual, private, brief — and they happen to the bereaved, not to skeptics (James) or enemies (Paul). There is no documented case of five hundred people sharing one hallucination. And no hallucination empties a tomb (Exhibit E).
6
THE LIE HYPOTHESIS SELF-DESTRUCTS
Yes, people die for false beliefs they think are true. But if they'd invented it, they'd have known it was a lie — and conspiracies unravel under pressure. This one grew under persecution, from ~120 (Acts 1:15) to an "immense multitude" in Rome by AD 64 (Tacitus, Exhibit B).
